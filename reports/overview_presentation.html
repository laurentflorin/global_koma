A multi-country Bayesian macro model

Structure, results and forecasts across five estimated systems

What this is

A multi-country Bayesian simultaneous-equations macro model, built on the koma package.

  • 11 euro-area economies + the US, quarterly, 2000–2024
  • Every country’s behavioural equations and accounting identities estimated in one system, so cross-country effects are jointly identified rather than bolted on afterwards
  • Five estimated systems, each a deliberate step: 1 → 2a → 2b → 2c → 2d, plus two extension blocks (3a, 3b)
  • Everything below comes from cached fits and is reproducible from the repo

The one thing that governs all of it: every system spends observations on parameters. df = T − k. Nearly every result in this deck is downstream of that number.

The country template — behavioural equations

Six equations per country, identical in form for every economy:

cc_consumption ~ cc_gdp + cc_long_rate + cc_consumption.L(1)
cc_investment ~ cc_gdp + cc_long_rate + cc_investment.L(1)
cc_exports ~ cc_foreign_demand + cc_exports.L(1)
cc_imports ~ cc_domestic_demand + cc_imports.L(1)
cc_prices ~ eur_usd + oil_price + cc_gdp + cc_prices.L(1)
cc_spread ~ cc_prices + cc_gdp + cc_spread.L(1)
  • Estimated in growth-rate space — coefficients on volumes are elasticities
  • long_rate, spread and the policy rates are rates: koma leaves them untransformed, so their coefficients are pp per pp
  • Four COVID impulse dummies (2020Q1–Q3, 2021Q2) enter the five real-side equations; suppressed above for readability

The country template — identities

Enforced during estimation, not fitted. Weights are fixed shares:

cc_gdp == 0.932*cc_domestic_demand + 0.406*cc_exports - 0.348*cc_imports
cc_domestic_demand == 0.731*cc_consumption + 0.269*cc_investment
cc_foreign_demand == 0.065*fr_imports + 0.052*it_imports + 0.098*us_imports + 0.104*cn_imports + 0.225*reu_imports + 0.456*row_gdp
cc_long_rate == 1*cc_spread + 1*ea_policy_rate
  • GDP — expenditure decomposition, exact only up to the statistical discrepancy
  • Domestic demand — absorption; government is out of the system entirely
  • Foreign demand — the trade-weighted external demand index exports load on. This is what links the countries. The weights shown are Germany’s; the partner set and shares are country-specific by construction
  • Long rate — a sovereign yield is the policy path plus a premium; both weights imposed at exactly 1

Plus, once per system

ea_policy_rate ~ ea_prices + ea_gdp + ea_policy_rate.L(1)
us_policy_rate ~ us_prices + us_gdp + us_policy_rate.L(1)   (stage 2b onward)
cn_policy_rate ~ cn_prices + cn_gdp + cn_policy_rate.L(1)   (stage 2d only)

ea_gdp    == sum_i g_i * <member i>_gdp
ea_prices == sum_i g_i * <member i>_prices

There is exactly one ea_policy_rate equation however many euro-area countries the system contains — that is what makes it a genuinely common instrument rather than ten separate ones.

Stages 1 and 2a — the mechanism

Stage 1 — each country its own small system, world variables exogenous.

Stage 2a — DE + FR in one system. Three things change, and they are the whole point:

  • exports driven by partner GDP, not an exogenous world aggregate
  • ea_policy_rate becomes endogenous
  • long_rate enters investment

The finding that set the agenda:

Making the policy rate endogenous did not give it a route to output. Without long_rate in investment it reached only the two long-rate equations — which are terminal.

“A rate rise cannot move GDP.”

Structure has to be traced, not assumed.

Stage 2b — all eleven, and an open loop

68 stochastic equations, 35 identities, k = 76, df = 22.

Tracing what ea_policy_rate can reach within a quarter:

91 of 103 endogenous variables — including every country’s GDP, and itself.

0 of 11 price variables.

The Taylor rule responds to an inflation rate that nothing it does can influence. The monetary block was an open loop, and no amount of estimation would have revealed it — only a structural trace did.

Stage 2c — closing the loop, for free

Four refinements, applied to every country. All four cost zero k — same k = 76, same df = 22 as stage 2b, so the two are directly comparable.

# Refinement Why
1 prices gains gdp the Phillips curve — closes the monetary loop
2 foreign_demand over partner imports partner GDP nets imports out, so it had the wrong sign
3 model the spread, long_rate becomes an identity policy-rate loading straddled zero in all 11
4 consumption gains long_rate a second monetary channel

Result: the policy rate now reaches all 114 endogenous variables, including every price. A fifth refinement was estimated and rejected — it halved in-sample RMSE while gutting the demand elasticity in 9 of 11 countries. Improved fit was the symptom, not the reassurance.

Stage 2c — and what closing the loop cost

In stage 2b prices had no contemporaneous endogenous regressor — it was effectively a univariate AR, and therefore structurally immune to explosive draws. Coupling it to GDP makes it reachable from the policy rate and from everything else that reaches GDP.

Use stage 2c for mechanism, stage 2b for a price fan chart.

Stage 2d — the first stage that buys degrees of freedom

Stage 2c’s equations, a re-partitioned world: DE, FR, IT, US separate, the other seven euro-area economies collapsed into a REU bloc, and China added.

Stage Change k df
Stage 2c 11 countries, refined 76 22
Stage 3a + German labour block 83 15
Stage 3b + external/fiscal/financial 90 8
Stage 2d re-partition to 6 entities 46 32

Every earlier structural change traded df for detail: 22 → 15 → 8. Collapsing seven countries removes enough equations that k falls 76 → 46 — so df rises to 32 even after giving up eighteen quarters of sample.

Stage 2d — China, and what the data does not have

What exists (free, keyless sources):

  • real GDP, SA — World Bank GEM
  • CPI — World Bank GEM
  • merchandise trade volumes — GEM value ÷ price
  • policy rate — OECD IRSTCI
  • trade weights — observed IMF DOTS

What does not exist, anywhere:

Quarterly consumption and investment.

Verified absent from OECD QNA, OECD Economic Outlook, IMF IFS, World Bank GEM, FRED and the NBS itself.

→ the two equations merge into one estimated cn_domestic_demand, rather than being invented from indicator proxies.

Two things stated rather than buried: cn_long_rate is spliced over 45% of the window, and reu_investment is not identified — Ireland’s investment sd is 43.0 and it carries 10.9% of the bloc, so the seven-country average is more volatile than any single member.

Stage 3a / 3b — the wall

Germany gets a labour/wage–price block, then external, fiscal and financial blocks on top.

Stage Adds k df
Stage 2b — 76 22
Stage 3a labour + disaggregated prices (DE only) 83 15
Stage 3b + external, fiscal, financial (DE only) 90 8
  • It cannot be rolled out — on eleven countries. The full block for all of them needs k = 142 against T = 98.
  • At df = 8, de_long_rate’s own lag crossed to 1.004 — explosive — the moment debt became its fifth regressor.
  • A quiet loop is not a stable one: the fiscal–financial cycle showed no explosive draws because one of its links was ~0.

Stage 3d — the rollout, on the regional core

Stage 3c’s verdict was that further structure needed a longer sample or a smaller system. Stage 2d built the smaller system; stage 3d puts the labour block on it.

Configuration Partition k df Economies covered
DE labour only 11 countries 83 15 1
DE + FR labour 11 countries 90 8 2
DE + REU labour 6 entities 60 18 8
DE + REU + FR + IT 6 entities 74 4 10

The block still costs seven columns per entity. What changed is what a column buys: a bloc covers seven economies for one entity’s worth of k.

And the aggregation is not only a cost. The wage Phillips curve is identified on the bloc (−0.176, interval excluding zero) and not on Germany (−0.105, straddling zero) — the bloc’s unemployment rate carries the crisis-era variation Germany’s does not. What is lost is the institutional coefficients: wage indexation and ULC pass-through both go wrong-signed on the bloc. The bloc has the variation but not the institutions; Germany the reverse.

Forecasts — how they are produced

Every system is run 8 quarters forward, 1000 posterior draws.

  • koma silently shortens a horizon whose exogenous series run out — they are extended first, and the delivered horizon is checked
  • koma constrains no draw to be stationary, so explosive draws are filtered and the discarded share is reported
  • Charts show an inner 25–75% band solid and an outer 10–90% band clipped to the panel edge; the untruncated bounds are tabulated
  • Stages 1 and 2a forecast from 2023Q1 (window already observed — a genuine out-of-sample check); stages 2b onward from 2025Q1

Forecast — stage 2d GDP

Forecast — stage 1, against the outturn

The realised path sits inside the band for every country, and the fan widens in exactly the countries you would expect.

The finding the forecasts made visible

Degrees of freedom are not an abstraction. They are exactly how much of the predictive distribution survives to horizon 8.

Spillovers — is any of it real?

Same fit, same battery, only the seed changed:

System Mean |A − B| Max Sign flips
Stage 2b (df 22) 0.350 1.850 7–10 of 30
Stage 2c (df 22) 0.119 0.356 1 of 44
Stage 2d (df 32) 0.108 0.273 0 of 20

For stage 2b, most of what anyone wanted to conclude was smaller than the matrix’s own noise.

Stage 2d is the first system where no off-diagonal cell changes sign when only the seed changes — the concrete payoff of those ten extra degrees of freedom.

What does not work

Check Result
Monetary → GDP (euro area) correctly signed, 6/6 peak negative
Monetary → prices wrong sign — only 1/6 peak negative
Monetary shock on China’s own rule does not work — a horizon-1 adjustment in the rule, not transmission
Oil → prices correctly signed 6/6
Spillover ~ bilateral trade weight fails — Spearman 0.138 against a 0.3 bar

The oil shock is the control: the price block responds sensibly to a cost shock and perversely to a policy rate. That localises the fault to the policy rate’s route in — not to the price equations.

Which system for which question

If you want to… Use
understand a mechanism / trace transmission Stage 2c
produce a price fan chart Stage 2b
study EA–US–China spillovers Stage 2d
say anything about Austria, Spain, Ireland… Stage 2b / 2c
model German wages, debt or credit Stage 3a / 3b — Germany only
run a monetary experiment on China nothing here yet

The through-line: every result in this deck that survived did so because it was checked against its own noise floor — a seed placebo, a sign rule, a structural trace, a second specification. The ones that did not survive are in the deck too.
